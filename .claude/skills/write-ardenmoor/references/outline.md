## HOW TO USE THIS OUTLINE

This outline is **rough scaffolding and inspiration, not a script.** It shows one strong path
through the book and the gravity the story moves under. The chapter beats below are candidate
scenes, not instructions. Widen freely: invent events not listed here, reorder or merge or split
what is, change the chapter count, follow a day the outline never mentions if the book wants it.
When the novel and the outline disagree, the novel wins.

Two things are **load-bearing** and must hold, because the ending depends on them:

- **The canon appendix** (bottom of this file) and `state/story-bible.md` — never contradict either.
- **The destinations (gravity wells):** the thesis (*ask for passage*) discovered early and earned
  at the climax; grief resolving in the order *letter read, then chest opened*; the compass
  pointing **down** as the closing twist; the two children's arcs converging on one formal

Everything else is **open**. Treat the beats as a well-stocked pantry, not a recipe.

## SERIES PREMISE

A grieving wizard buys a hill no one has ever managed to build on and slowly raises a tower there,
not as a fortress but as a home. Each book covers a stretch of the tower's growth and the deepening
mystery of the hill beneath it, which is old, reactive, and had been answering nothing in particular
long before he arrived. The series arc: the hill is a knot in the grain and so sits against a door,
an existing potential plane, and the wizard is the only living person who has built the instrument
that can find one. (Both wordings here were retired 2026-08-08. The old "aware" implied a being,
which the hill is not; and the old chosen-one phrasing of who could open the door is retired because
the destiny lock forbids it. Both patterns are in `tools/superseded.txt`.) The cozy register never
breaks; the stakes grow by becoming more *personal*, not more apocalyptic.

**Book One spans TWO YEARS** (locked 2026-07-14, session-lock #10): arrival in the spring of Year
One through the winter solstice of Year Two. The first buildings, the first tenants, the village's
slow turn from suspicion to belonging, two children discovering what they are, one formal
apprenticeship, a fire, and a locked chest finally opened.

## LOGLINE

After the death of his fiancée, the wizard Emlyn buys a hill that hums at the far end of a
Commonwealth frontier, builds a life there one careful room at a time against a debt that must be
answered, and learns that a village and a hill can only be won the same way ground is worked: not
by asking for level, but by asking for passage. When his instruments reveal the hill has been
*greeting* something in his cellar for thirty days, he must decide whether the locked chest he has
not touched since her death is the thing being called, or whether he is.

## THESIS & THEMES

The controlling idea, from an old surveyor's marginal note (found Ch 4, earned at the climax):

> *Do not ask for level. Ask for passage. The ground knows the difference.*

Every arc is a variation on it. Supporting themes: teaching as healing; being known (the hill
learning Emlyn's shape mirrors the village learning his character); shared weight (Tomas carrying
the hum alone vs. together); the domestic as sacred; **and the wood-first lesson: on this hill,
what is thrown up cheap does not endure — only what is asked for, and built to be answered for.**

## THE CAST (with one-line arcs; ages span the two years)

- **Emlyn** — planewright wizard, precise, patient, quietly funny; past forty and wears it young.
  Arc: from a man storing his grief in a locked chest to a man who opens it. His late **fiancée
  Arielle** (they were never married; the river house was his wedding gift, never lived in) exists
  only in objects: the chest, the callipers in the sticking drawer, the shelf gap, her mother's
  unopened letter.
- **Soren** (~9 at arrival; 11 by the finale) — Cael's grandson; hears the hill. Arc: watcher at
  the property line → the tower's first informal student. His drawing captioned *"the hill's good
  place"* is the book's emotional midpoint.
- **Ren** (~16 the first summer; ~18 at apprenticeship) — Jorin's son; feels magic through his
  hands. Arc: crew boy with a knack → the tower's first *formal* apprentice.
- **Jorin** — master builder; single words that carry paragraphs. Arc: entrusts Emlyn with
  buildings, then with his son.
- **Tomas** — blacksmith; heard the hum alone for months and told no one. Arc: solitary carrier →
  first confidant. His standing promise (*he will know when Emlyn knows*) ticks through the book.
- **Brenna** — herbalist; inheritor of her grandmother's forty years of listening. Arc: courtesy →
  true partnership (the greenhouse). Custodian of the **keeper plant**.
- **Lira** — innkeeper; measured, gives nothing unpaid-for; the village's slowest thaw, and (reader
  learns in Part III, Emlyn much later) an informal Conclave informant.
- **Cael** — VILLAGE ELDER (locked 2026-07-14): brokered the assembled sale on the families' behalf;
  sold his own family's hill only to a man who'd make something of it. Late 50s, vaguely rendered.
- **Penworth** — Emlyn's dry, immaculate man of affairs (~a decade of service; ~Emlyn's age; a
  minor talent, slightly long-lived). Engine of the outside-world subplot; forced the river-house
  sale. His daughter **Nerys** is seeded for Book Two.
- **The Wrays** — Hal and Bess, first tenants of the bottoms (Ch 9): fair terms, hard hands; their
  first harvest is Part III's proof the land can pay.
- Supporting: **Edran** (quarry), **Maret** (mill), **Kellard** (yard), **Essa** (furniture),
  **Cecily** (investigator, letters only), **Drace** (Conclave official, offstage shadow).

## THE FOUR BRAIDED ARCS

**A. Building & the ledger (external spine):** hill → stone Study → timber barn + cabin (cheap and
fast under the note — the seeded mistake) → the CELLAR cut beneath the Study (Ch 12; the chest moves
down into its NE corner) → greenhouse → storehouse cut INTO the hill → the road
and its village branch → the lab → **the fire** → (Book Two: the stone rebuild, the tower proper).
Every structure is also a relationship, and every season answers to the loan clock: grace ends in
Year 2; the river-house money pays down principal mid-book; tenants and the lamp trade make the
place pay. Commerce is always also character.

**B. Apprentices (heart):** Soren's Wednesdays running parallel to Ren's slower discovery,
converging at the Part IV ceremony with Soren standing witness.

**C. Grief (interior):** domestic thresholds only — the chest untouched, the letter unopened, the
drawer left stuck, her things crated under seal rather than sorted. Resolution Ch 28–29: letter,
then chest.

**D. Undercurrent (mystery):** the hum → Tomas's confession → Deep Grammar Phase One (the hill is
learning him) → the three correspondents and the unsigned hand → the Lira chapter → Phase Two (the
greeting, toward the chest's corner) → "the office remains interested" → the needle. Weather, not
storm, until Part IV.

## PART II — REMAINING BEATS (Ch 11–14; Ch 8–10 are drafted)

**Ch 10 — The Wrays.** *(NEW, 2026-07-15 — split out of Breaking Ground at the author's request; all
later chapters shift +1.)* Spring Yr2, stepping back from the house. Emlyn cannot farm, so he goes to
**Cael**, who names **Hal and Bess Wray**. The tenancy negotiation plays as a two-hander (Hal: "There's
a year's work in it before it gives you a bushel"; Bess from the doorway: "A fifth." and "Men find
reasons"; answered with the paper and Cael). They come across on one borrowed cart. **THEN THE SET-UP
THAT THE WHOLE TENANCY ARC PAYS OFF:** a fortnight later Hal comes up the track on a Sunday with his
hat in his hands and tells Emlyn what is actually on his own land — **a three-acre strip of Tobin
Hask's straight through the middle of the best field**, a widow's cottage corner, two meadow acres held
by a stranger, and **thirty acres gone back to thistle because the only lane to them runs through a
gate somebody wired shut a decade ago over a forgotten quarrel**. Hal does not ask him to fix it; he
comes so Emlyn cannot later say he ploughed it badly. *"It's a rare mess down there… You've the whole
of it in one hand now."* Emlyn says he will look into it, **puts it on a list, and does not touch it
for eighteen months** — "Hal had said *people* and I had heard *paperwork*, and paperwork can wait, and
people, as it turned out, had been waiting eleven years already."

**Ch 11 — The Listener and the Singer. ✅ DRAFTED 2026-07-14.** Emlyn goes DOWN to Brenna's cottage
and reads the FIRST of the grandmother's notebooks (lent ONE VOLUME AT A TIME); sees the keeper
plant in the cold cellar (3 new leaves since he took the hill); candor stays PARTIAL (keeps the hum
private). What LANDS on the page is only the grandmother's early-book line: the hill sings, the tree
only holds it and passes it on, and it is not for her. The theory's SPINE (the Singer sings to

**Ch 12 — What Tomas Carried.** The confession chapter. Late pickup, banked forge; Tomas has heard
the hill for many months and told no one ("who tells such a thing?"). The relief of not being mad,
spoken by a man who wastes nothing. The standing promise made: *when I know what it is, you will
know.* The book's quiet metronome starts.

**Ch 13 — The Listening Stone.** The Deep Grammar program begins, and first the DIG: Jorin's crew
cuts the CELLAR beneath the Study (reshaping wards asking, not insisting; the hill opening for it as
it opened for the footing), stairs down from the Study, dry stone, the hill's cold breath. During the
dig Ren goes still over the NORTHEAST corner and says nothing, and Jorin sees it and says nothing
either (this is the moment Ch 20's 'He felt something in that cellar. During the build.' refers to;
keep it BLIND on the page). When the room is closed in, Emlyn carries the CHEST down himself and sets
it against the northeast corner, the driest wall, the hill's corner again, the same unexamined
choosing as the Study. Then the program proper: a crystalline recording blank set flush in the cellar
floor, thirty days of sub-harmonics; Ren sets stones without instruction, each exactly true; Jorin
notices; the reader notices Jorin noticing. **River-house completion papers arrive: the money
lands and goes straight at the principal; her things are crated unsorted under Penworth's seal and
stored. He signs where the flags are. He does not read the inventory.**

**Ch 14 — The Well Incident.** Soren's talent slips in public: water climbing against the rope's
tug, a heartbeat of wrongness in the square. Emlyn smooths it to nothing with a wizard's
misdirection. Lira, wiping a table by the window, sees more than she says. The boy must be
protected from being *noticed*.

**Ch 15 — Midsummer Reading.** Phase One read: two pulses daily; storm-sensitivity; a response the
day he visited the oak; and across thirty days a baseline warming — the hill is *learning him*.
Penworth visits (met at Wainford or come the whole way): the summer's mail holds three strangers
probing the deliberately incomplete demiplane papers, one unsigned ("*the interior architecture is
beautiful*"); Cecily engaged. **The first loan payment is made and survived — tight, but made; the
tenants and the lamps did it.** **INTERLUDE II** — *predawn, the hill says the shape of him back to
the dark.*

**Ch 17 — The Rent Book.** *(NEW, 2026-07-15, author's call; all chapters after shift +1.)* Autumn Yr2,

## PART IV — YEAR 3, SPRING → SOLSTICE (Ch 20–29; timeline confirmed 2026-07-28: glasshouse finishes midwinter Yr2→3, thaw = spring Yr3, fire early winter Yr3, needle at solstice Yr3)

**Ch 26 — Keeping the Promise.** Tomas gets the true shape (not everything; the chest and compass
stay Emlyn's): the hill answers, it has been learning his hand, and it was doing so long before
either of them came; Tomas was never mad and never alone. "Waiting for what?" — "I don't know yet.

**Ch 27 — The Delicate Conversation.** The apprenticeship negotiation, the relational climax.
Jorin's conditions in his own grammar: the boy finishes what he builds; the boy is not taken from

"Yes. I've been answering it for a year. I'd like to know what I'm saying."

**Ch 28 — The Keeper Plant.** Warm-glow chapter before the dark: the greenhouse alive; Brenna sets
her grandmother's plant in true ground for the first time in thirty years; by morning its leaves
have turned — toward the hill. The council votes to widen the wizard's way into a cart-road at the village's own charge. Lira puts a Series lamp in
her window.

**Ch 29 — The Lab.** The storehouse finished, the hill holding the rear wall like a promise; the
hill-cut inner chamber becomes the enchantment lab. The apprenticeship made formal the old way —
small, domestic, binding; bread broken; Soren standing witness with the fierce arithmetic of a boy
computing his own future. Ren's first supervised working: a lamp housing accepting its light as he
stops pushing and starts asking.

**Ch 30 — THE FIRE (new; placement locked 2026-07-14: before the finale).** An early-winter night;
an ordinary cause the morning never quite settles (a lamp, a dry month, a flue) — the timber barn
goes, and the cabin with it; Snow and what matters saved; the hill blameless on the page and the
seeds (she never would settle there) left for the reader's hindsight. THE VILLAGE COMES UP THE HILL
— buckets, blankets, bread; belonging proven in ash. He winters back in the Study among his
instruments, beside the cellar. The money arithmetic goes hard again; rebuilding must wait for
spring, "and in stone this time," which is all he says of it. **In the ashes' aftermath, Cecily's
letter:** the two named correspondents traced and warned off; the unsigned hand untraceable, the
letters stopped, and one line that closes his throat — the hand shows training, *his* school of
notation, the kind of closeness that would have required her unguarded rooms in the three years
they sat unentered before the crating.

**Ch 31 — The Letter.** Grief resolves before the twist, so the twist lands on an open heart.
Solstice eve, the Study, the fire's smell still in everything he owns: he opens Arielle's mother's
letter. It is short. It asks him to come in spring, and to bring "whatever of her still lives in
your keeping." He sits with it until the lumen is the only light in the valley. Then he takes the
lamp, and the key he has carried for two years, and goes down the cellar stairs.

**Ch 32 — The Needle.** The twist, predawn at the winter solstice, the hill's deepest pulse almost
a word. The chest opened at last: wrapped in her scarf, the Planewright's Compass, waking at his
touch for the first time since her hands last held it. The needle turns once, settles, and points
straight down. The hill has never been greeting the compass. It has been waiting for someone to
notice the door. Final image: frost flowering on the cellar vent; in the hill-cut lab the small
steady glow of an apprentice's first enchantment; in a grieving man's open hand, a needle pointing
down. **INTERLUDE IV / CODA** — *the hill, one line: at last.*

## THE TWIST, UNPACKED

1. **Mystery payoff (primary):** the compass points down — recontextualizing every hum, pulse, and
   greeting. The hill is adjacent to an existing potential plane; the "waiting" the grandmother
   theorized is the door waiting to be noticed. The series engine.
2. **Emotional payoff:** the chest opens *because* the letter was read. Passage, not level.
3. **Tension hook (unresolved):** the unsigned trained hand; her crated pages now under seal but
   unguarded for three years first; the spring summons. Book Two opens toward the city. The reader
   closes the book warm and worried in exactly one spot.

## BOOK TWO SETUP (seeded, not opened)

Spring, Year 3: **the stone rebuild** (barn and guest cabin raised again, right this time — "only
stone endures on this hill") runs alongside **the journey to the city**: the crates unsealed at
last, Arielle's mother, the third spellbook and the unsigned hand, Penworth and Nerys, Drace's
courtesy visit — and beneath the tower, the question the series now orbits: what do you do with a
door the ground itself has been asking you to open?

## CANON APPENDIX (locked facts — do not contradict)

**⚠️ THE GRAIN AND THE KNOT (author, 2026-08-08 — what is under the hill; full text in the story bible):** magic is ambient and everywhere in roughly even distribution, and in any place it lies along that place's **grain**. Almost everywhere the grain is inert. **Where it converges you get a knot**: a point hypersensitive and reactive to workings. A knot is a **phenomenon, not a being** — it reacts, it never intends or waits for anyone. That reactivity is the old instability: push a knot and it gives the pushing back, which is why nothing ever stayed set on that crown.
