### The style guide governs the sentences; this file governs the story. Where they seem to

### owes the reader pull, cause, and payoff — it just pays them without raising its voice.

## HOW TO USE THIS FILE

Sixteen modules. Module 0 is the register every other module inherits. Modules 1–4A are
structural and narrational law (enforced in the plan block and checklists). Modules 5–12
are technique (consulted while drafting, checked in revision). Modules 13–14 are process.
When any rule here collides with a session-locked author decision, the lock wins and the
collision is logged.

The degree's bookshelf, for orientation (principles are absorbed below; the names are for
tracing a rule to its source): Swain, *Techniques of the Selling Writer* (scene/sequel,
MRUs) · Gardner, *The Art of Fiction* (psychic distance) · Maass, *The Emotional Craft of
Fiction* and *The Fire in Fiction* (microtension, emotional craft) · Sanderson's plot
lectures (promise/progress/payoff, try-fail) · Le Guin, *Steering the Craft* (rhythm,
POV) · Baxter, *The Art of Subtext* · Coyne, *The Story Grid* (value shifts) · King,
*On Writing* (revision) · the cozy-fantasy working canon (Baldree, Klune, Chambers,
Mandanna) read as craft, not comfort — and never as prose models: they are comps for
warmth *mechanics* only; where they are chatty, whimsical, or sweet, this book is still,
dry, and grave.

## MODULE 0 — THE HOUSE REGISTER: WHAT COZY MEANS IN THIS BOOK

Every module below inherits this calibration. When any technique has a loud execution and
a quiet one, this book takes the quiet one, always.

- **Cozy played straight, and grown.** No whimsy engine: no cute creatures, no
  exclamatory warmth, no twee. Comfort here is generated by precision, labor, weather,
  courtesy, and things done properly — the exact sentence about the kettle IS the
  coziness. The mandated comic beat is dry and structural (a child's category-audit, a
  toll joke, Penworth's four sentences), never zany. If a drafted beat could appear in a
  chatty cozy unchanged, re-voice it or cut it.
- **The content covenants** (what the reader is guaranteed, and the manuscript already
  keeps): no on-page violence, gore, or cruelty; no sexual content; deaths happen
  before page one or offstage; animals are safe — in a catastrophe, the horse is saved
  first and the saving is shown; a child's jeopardy is only ever social (being noticed,
  being taken from a craft), never physical; no villain gets a body on stage — menace
  arrives as paper, seals, and handwriting. Breaking a covenant is not "raising
  stakes"; it is leaving the genre.
- **No romance line unless the author opens one.** Warmth with every character stays
  companionate; the author has already cut one accidental romance-whisper from the
  draft, and that cut is a standing lock. This book's love story is grief, and its
  courtship is a village deciding to keep a man. Never drift a romance in through
  lingering description or loaded beats; if a scene's warmth starts reading romantic,
  re-aim it at the friendship's own register.
- **Underplay is the reaction system.** In this book the visible response to enormous
  things is characteristically the smallest legal one — a hammer's half-beat pause, a
  drawer let stay stuck, "Even." The withheld reaction IS the reaction, and the reader
  is trusted to feel its size. Techniques below that generate visceral response chains
  are calibrated accordingly (Module 2).
- **Friction's ceiling is a refusal.** Hostility is not in this palette. The maximum
  interpersonal temperature is a boundary held, a bargain driven hard, a subject closed,
  a door not opened — and those must do all the work hostility does elsewhere.
- **Stillness is a deliverable, not a defect.** Silence, weather, and slow work rendered
  with full attention are what the reader came for. The modules exist to put CURRENT
  under the stillness — microtension, carried questions, sub-ledgers — never to replace
  stillness with event. A revision that fixes a "slow" chapter by adding incident has
  misread this file.
- **The house ending shape:** warmth on the page, wonder underneath it, and exactly one
  aimed worry carried out the door. Not warmth alone (unearned), not worry alone
  (wrong genre).

## MODULE 1 — STORY PHYSICS: PROMISE, PROGRESS, PAYOFF

Every unit of story runs on three currents (Sanderson's frame, and the oldest law there
is): a **promise** opened, **progress** felt, a **payoff** that answers the promise in a
way both surprising and inevitable.

- **Promises are made at every scale.** The book promises (a tower, a thaw, a chest, a
  hum explained). Each part promises (a build, a debt, an assessment). Each chapter
  promises within its first page — a task, a visit, a letter — and the chapter is judged
  by whether it answers or deliberately converts that promise.
- **Progress must be *felt*, not merely occur.** The job is not to make things advance
  but to make the reader sense satisfying movement. In this book progress is measured in
  physical and social ledgers the reader can check: courses of stone, entries in
  Penworth's column, words Lira has never said before, the specificity of the hill's
  voice. When a span feels static, the defect is almost never missing events — it is
  missing *visible measures*. Add a ledger the reader can read, not an event.
- **Payoff = promise + pressure + angle.** A payoff that answers the promise exactly as
  posed is flat; one that ignores it is a cheat. The needle pointing down is the model:
  every promise about the hum is answered, at the promised place and hour, from an angle
  no reader posed. Test every major payoff against that trident: promised? pressured?
  angled?
- **The try–fail cycle, cozy-tuned.** Progress toward anything worth having runs through
  failures that escalate (the dark lamp before the LUMEN, the arrears that would not pay
  a crew for a week). The cozy calibration: failures cost money, time, pride, and
  comfort — never safety of the soul. Use **yes-but / no-and** on every attempt: a clean
  "yes" is allowed roughly once per part, and it should land where the reader has earned
  it. One licensed exception: the opening movement runs warmer by design — the book's own
  doctrine is to let the reader fall for the world before the plot asks anything — and
  there the ration relaxes, with the quiet resistances (the note, the ground, the chest,
  the weather) supplying friction enough.

## MODULE 2 — SCENE DESIGN: THE UNIT THAT DOES THE WORK

Swain's mechanics, translated. A scene is an attempt: **goal → resistance → outcome**.
What follows it is the sequel: **reaction → dilemma → decision**, and the decision is the
next scene's cause. This alternation IS the causality law — "therefore/but, never
and-then" is scene-and-sequel viewed from above.

House translations for a cozy literary book:

- **"Disaster" becomes "the turn."** Swain ends scenes on disaster; this book ends them
  on a shift — a complication, a cost, a door opened a hand's width. The mechanism is
  identical (the outcome must change the situation); only the volume differs. An outcome
  that leaves the situation exactly as the scene found it means the scene was a
  photograph.
- **The value shift (Story Grid's contribution).** Before drafting, name the value the
  chapter moves and its polarity at both ends: stranger→neighbor, sealed→cracked,
  solvent→pledged, unheard→almost-a-word. If start and end read the same polarity, find
  the shift or find the reason the stillness is itself the shift (a refusal-beat is a
  legal shift: attended→deliberately-unattended).
- **Sequels are where this book lives — but they are earned, not free.** The
  reaction-dilemma-decision passage is the natural home of the teller's interiority, and
  the finale license caps how many may run consecutively. Every sequel must end in a
  decision the next chapter spends; a sequel that ends in mood alone is a lyric essay
  wearing a chapter's clothes.
- **Enter late, leave early.** Open as near the chapter's live pressure as the anchor
  paragraph allows; end on the image *before* the explanation. The settle-image closes
  the scene; it does not summarize it.
- **Cause before effect, down to the sentence.** Swain's MRU: the stimulus lands on the
  page before the response, and the response runs feeling → reflex → considered
  action/speech. In practice this is a line-edit law: never "He stepped back, because the
  wall shifted" — the wall shifts, then he steps back. The reader's nervous system keeps
  this order even when the syntax doesn't, and every inversion costs a half-beat of
  trust. Interiority may compress the chain; it may not reverse it. House calibration
  (per Module 0): the chain's ORDER is law, its VOLUME is not — in this book the visible
  response is usually the smallest legal one, a pause or a set-down tool, and the full
  feeling-reflex-speech chain is reserved for the handful of crisis beats (the fire, the
  lock ending) where the book permits itself to be loud for a sentence.

## MODULE 3 — MICROTENSION: THE PAGE-LEVEL CURRENT

Maass's finding, and the single most important tool for a low-stakes book: readers keep
turning pages not because of what might happen in the story but because of unease about
what might happen *in the next few lines*. Microtension is generated by **conflicting
currents held in one moment** — emotions, loyalties, or intentions that pull two ways.

This book's native supply is enormous; the craft is spending it line by line:

- **In dialogue:** friction beneath courtesy. Two people who like each other wanting
  slightly different things from the same conversation (every Jorin negotiation; Brenna
  refusing the north corner's reason). Check any flat exchange: what does each speaker
  want the *other* not to notice?
- **In description:** description tenses when it is focalized through a want or a worry.
  The same greenhouse is inventory when the narrator is idle and a wager when the note is
  due. Never describe from nowhere; the camera always has a stake.
- **In interiority:** contradictory feeling is the engine — grateful for the fire's
  occupation, proud of a boy he must hide, glad of a sale that guts him. One conflicted
  clause outperforms a paragraph of consistent feeling. When a passage sags, do not add
  event; add the second, opposing current.
- **In exposition:** information tenses when someone on the page needs it, fears it, or
  is owed it. The Cecily material moved from recital to dread the moment "who had leisure
  in that house" aimed it at a person.
- **The audit:** on revision, open to any page cold and ask what, on *this* page, an
  attentive reader is uneasy about. The answer "nothing, but the prose is lovely" is a
  finding.

## MODULE 4 — PSYCHIC DISTANCE: THE CAMERA WITH A CONSCIENCE

Gardner's spectrum, from remote ("It was the third week of spring") to fused with the
character's consciousness, with every step between. Rules of use:

- **Move by steps, not jumps.** A jolt from panorama to inner nerve in one sentence
  breaks the dream. Open chapters a step or two out (the anchor paragraph is naturally
  mid-distance), close in as the pressure rises, and step back out for the settle.
- **Depth is a resource; spend it at thresholds.** If every paragraph runs at maximum
  closeness, closeness stops meaning anything. The book's biggest moments (the lock
  ending, the needle) EARN full fusion because the surrounding narration holds a
  courteous middle distance. This is the mechanical truth under the style guide's "the
  prose gets quieter at the biggest moments": quieter = closer, with less commentary.
- **The retrospective teller adds a second axis: distance in TIME.** This narrator can
  stand beside his younger self (near hindsight: "I did not know then") or inside him
  (the events as lived). The house doctrine: live INSIDE during scenes; step BESIDE only
  in sequels, and never to predict. Hindsight may weight a moment ("I have turned that
  evening over more than most"); it may not promise one ("little did I know") — the
  telegraph ban, stated as camera law.
- **Diagnostic:** any passage that feels flat is usually at the wrong distance, not the
  wrong length — most often too far out during pressure, too far in during logistics.
  Re-aim before rewriting.

## MODULE 4A — THE WITNESS POSITION: POINT OF VIEW IN THE LE GUIN LINE

Point of view in this book is not a camera setting; it is a moral stance. The lineage is
Le Guin's — with Marilynne Robinson (*Gilead*), Wendell Berry (*Jayber Crow*, *Hannah
Coulter*), Kent Haruf (*Plainsong*), and Robin Hobb's Fitz memoirs as the working kin: a
narrator who is a WITNESS, a member of the place he tells about, rendering what he
attended to with the gravity of someone answerable for the account. Le Guin's first law
in *Steering the Craft* — that point of view is the fundamental choice and its
consistency is the reader's ground — is already house canon. What this module adds is
her deeper teaching: whose eyes the story uses decides whose dignity the story confers.

- **The narrator attends; he does not preside.** He is in the account, of the place,
  below the events — never above them handing down verdicts. Irony points inward first
  and always hardest at himself ("rather more skill than sense"). Toward others the
  license is AFFECTIONATE wryness, which the manuscript uses constantly and rightly: the
  valley's "sideways admiration," Kellard's mirror he "had by him," Ferris thinking he
  swindled a wizard. The test that separates it from smirk: the sentence exposes a small
  vanity the valley shares or pays the person a kindness in the same breath, and its
  subject, hearing it read aloud in Lira's taproom, would grin or shrug — not go quiet.
  What is banned is contempt: any sentence whose subject would go quiet is out of
  position. This is the Le Guin practice exactly: in Earthsea the narration confers
  seriousness on goatherds and archmages alike, and that impartial gravity is where the
  books' authority comes from.
- **Every person gets their best angle — without prettifying.** When someone must be
  shown at their worst (Hask, twice refusing), the page also carries their reason, their
  history, or their side's arithmetic, because everyone is the protagonist of their own
  account. The Hask standard is the model: the narration indicts the narrator's own
  "small ugly thing," never the man who was maneuvered. Judgment in this book is shown
  as consequence and self-audit; it is never delivered as a narration verdict on
  another character.
- **Strict epistemic honesty (the first-person contract).** The teller knows only what
  he saw, was told, worked out, or has since learned — and the seams show. Another mind
  is never asserted, only read: evidence first, inference visibly owned ("I read it as,"
  "I took it for," "which was Jorin knighting him"). The reader must always be able to
  distinguish what happened from what the teller made of it; that visible distinction is
  the voice's honesty, and half its intimacy. When later knowledge fills a gap, the
  filling is dated ("I know now from her own mouth") — the memoir equivalent of citing
  a source.
- **The involved teller's latitude (Le Guin's defense of the telling voice).** Against
  the workshop dogma of pure dramatization, Le Guin holds that a narrating voice may
  generalize, reflect, and speak proverb — that the SOUND of a story being told is a
  legitimate instrument, not a failure of showing. This book uses that latitude ("a man
  has to be sent somewhere"), and keeps it honest with the existing rations: the tic
  budgets, the weld rule, and the standard that an aphorism earns its place by rising
  out of the concrete instance on the page, never descending onto it from stock.
- **The third-person chapters keep the same ethics.** A chapter in another's close third
  (Lira's) is not a break from the witness position but its extension: limited to her
  perceptions, rendered with the same impartial gravity, interior only as far as her own
  idiom reaches (ledgers, drafts, drawers), and utterly without authorial smirk. The
  test for any such chapter: its subject could read it and, whatever it cost her, could
  not call it unfair.
- **Things and ground have standing.** In this lineage landscape is not backdrop but a
  party to the story — Le Guin's islands, Berry's Port William membership, this book's
  hill. The interludes are the formal expression (the detached-yet-attending eye,
  wordless and patient), but the standing applies everywhere: tools, weather, rooms,
  and ground are attended to as things with histories and, in this book, one of them
  with intentions. The narration never uses a made thing or a landscape merely as
  scenery for a feeling; it gives the thing its own sentence first.
- **The membership, told from inside.** Berry's contribution: the village is a body the
  narrator belongs to, and the account's warmth comes from writing as a member — debts
  remembered, kindnesses dated, the community's verdicts reported in its own grammar
  ("the valley in its kindness spent that whole winter settling it in six directions on
  my behalf"). The narrator reports what the village decided as a weather system he
  lives inside, not a phenomenon he studies.
- **Attention as the moral act (Robinson's contribution).** In *Gilead* the quality of
  the noticing — light on water, an old man's hands — carries the book's whole theology
  without argument. Here likewise: the exactness of attention IS the book's ethic, and
  the narrator's growth registers as a widening of what he troubles to see (from load
  paths, to people, to what a boy's stillness holds). When the account must say
  something large, it says it by looking at something small for one sentence longer
  than economy requires.
- **Self-correction as the frame's conscience** (extends Module 12): in this lineage
  the retrospective narrator audits his own fairness on the page — Robinson's Ames
  catching his uncharity, this teller catching the mirror turned to the wall. Schedule
  it; the account that never doubts itself forfeits the trust the witness position
  exists to earn.

Enforcement: the plan block's sub-ledger line gains a sibling check at post-flight —
no narration verdict on another character, no unowned inference about another mind, and
any person shown at their worst got their reason on the page.

## MODULE 5 — EMOTIONAL CRAFT: MAKING THE READER FEEL, NOT WATCH FEELING

The reader's emotion is the product; the character's emotion is only one ingredient
(Maass's core reversal). Three modes, chosen deliberately per beat:

- **Outer mode (shown):** behavior implies feeling; the reader names it. The book's
  default and the restraint doctrine's home. Its risk is inertness when the behavior is
  too neutral to imply anything — shown emotion still needs a legible vector.
- **Inner mode (told, freshly):** the narrator reports feeling in words no one has used
  before. Telling is legal in this voice when the telling is itself an act of precision
  ("part cowardice, part mercy" is told emotion, and it is the best line in its chapter).
  Banned: told emotion in received language.
- **The third path (the strongest): report everything AROUND the feeling and omit the
  feeling itself.** The turned-down corner of the wallpaper book names no emotion; the
  reader supplies grief at higher intensity than any sentence could set. When a beat
  matters most, ask what can be *removed* so the reader must produce the feeling
  themselves. The reader's own generated emotion is the only kind that lasts past the
  page.
- **Emotional range check, per part:** grief, warmth, and wonder are the book's staples;
  a part should also touch at least three of — pride, shame, envy (Soren's arithmetic),
  fear, anger banked into courtesy, and being embarrassingly, unaccountably happy. A
  book that feels "samey" is usually range-poor, not event-poor.

## MODULE 6 — DIALOGUE & SUBTEXT: THE UNSAID DOES THE LIFTING

Baxter's law: the scene's real subject is the one nobody names. Working rules:

- **Two ledgers per exchange.** Every conversation has a text (lamp collars, soil plans)
  and a sub-ledger (trust, worry, an old debt). Plan both. A scene whose sub-ledger is
  empty is a transaction and belongs in summary.
- **Speech acts, not information transfer.** Each line DOES something — offers, tests,
  shields, concedes, refuses, forgives. In revision, verb every line of a key scene; any
  line whose only verb is "informs" gets cut or loaded.
- **Answer the question beneath the question.** Characters in this book habitually reply
  one level down ("Waiting for what?" answered with a promise, not a fact). Preserve
  this; it is the dialogue system's signature.
- **The oblique refusal.** Nobody on this page says "I don't want to talk about it."
  They change the subject with their hands, answer a different question, or reach for
  work. Direct refusals are spent perhaps twice a book, where they land like blows.
- **Voice-lock plus PRESSURE-lock.** The fixed speech rules say how each person talks;
  add how each *argues*: Jorin narrows to terms; Tomas goes stiller; Brenna completes
  her sentence no matter what; Lira itemizes; Penworth escalates formality; Cael comes
  further sideways. Conflict must not flatten idiom — under pressure, idiom intensifies.

### Diction domains — how to make voices distinct without dialect

Give each recurring character a **domain of metaphor** drawn from their work, and let them reach for
it under pressure. This is the cheapest and most durable characterization device available, and it
never reads as an accent.

- **Jorin** measures. Load, bearing, plumb, square, what a thing will carry. His verdicts are
  single words because a measurement does not need a sentence.
- **Ren** thinks in frames and loads, but he is learning a second vocabulary; his best lines are the
  moment his old domain reaches something new ("it's built true and it stands other").
- **Tomas** works in heat and tolerance. He answers in three words or fewer, and his real answers are
  physical: a hammer that pauses half a beat, an arm out straight.
- **Brenna** grows things. She measures in seasons and in what a living thing will tolerate. Flat,
  exact, no ceremony.
- **Penworth** accounts. Columns, dates, liabilities, the sentence eleven lines long. His warmth
  arrives disguised as scolding.
- **Cael** governs. He speaks in precedent, procedure, and the management of other people's opinions.
- **Lira** weighs. She gives nothing unpaid-for, and her diction is the ledger's: what a thing costs,
  what it is worth, what is owed.
- **Soren** counts. He is a child who reports observations and withholds inference.

## MODULE 7 — TIME: SCENE, SUMMARY, AND THE ITERATIVE

Three gears, each with a job:

- **Full scene** for anything that changes a relationship, pays a promise, or turns a
  part. If the ledger will ever say "paid off," it happens in this gear.
- **Summary** for repeatable process and travel — but summary in this voice is never
  dead time; it is the teller's essay gear, and it must earn its length with observation,
  not chronology. Anchor by day-labels and physical progress, never "time passed."
- **The iterative scene** (the Wednesdays gear): narrating the shape of many days as one
  day. This is the cozy genre's secret weapon — ritual rendered once, richly, then
  varied. Rules: establish the template in full scene once; thereafter only the
  variations are narrated; and when the ritual finally BREAKS (a missed Wednesday, bread
  from another hand), the break lands as event, because the template made it legible.
  Every established ritual is a tension instrument waiting to be played by omission.
- **Dilation at thresholds.** The most important thirty seconds of a part gets the most
  words-per-second in the part (the lock ending "the way a held breath ends" is the
  model). Inversely: never dilate logistics. The words-per-second curve across a chapter
  should map onto its pressure curve; in revision, find the paragraph where they diverge.

## MODULE 8 — DESCRIPTION & SETTING AS NARRATIVE

- **Double duty, always.** Description advances character, mood, or plot while it
  describes — the frost on the vent in the final image is weather, season-clock, and the
  world going on regardless, in nine words. A descriptive passage doing only one job is
  half-built.
- **Focalization is non-negotiable** (see Module 3): what the narrator notices IS
  characterization. A draftsman notices load paths and tolerances; a grieving man's eye
  routes around certain corners; catalog only what this man, today, would see.
- **Rhythm is meaning** (Le Guin's territory): sentence length and stress should enact
  the content — long unbroken syntax for continuous labor, short declaratives at
  impact, the settle-image's cadence slowing like a door coming to rest. Read closes
  aloud in the head; a settle-image with a galloping rhythm isn't settled.
- **The setting is a character with an arc.** The hill has one (indifference → attention
  → greeting → word); so does the village (receipt-warmth → neighborhood → the lantern
  chain); so does the tower itself (tent → Study → the lit valley). Track their arcs in
  the bible like people. Setting beats can carry a chapter's turn.

### The authority of the specific

The reason this book can carry an aware hill is that it can carry a glass gauge.

A reader extends trust earned on verifiable ground to unverifiable ground. When the mortar cures in
three days, the fall on a cold-store floor is one in sixty, and a lamp costs a half-gold, the reader
concludes that this narrator knows things — and then accepts the hum on the same credit. Specificity
is not decoration; it is the collateral on which the fantasy borrows.

**Practical rules.**

- Prefer the exact to the evocative. "Eleven fleeces" beats "a quantity of wool."
- The specific must be *load-bearing*, not merely present. A number nobody acts on is set-dressing.
- Errors are expensive in both directions: a wrong specific costs more trust than a vague statement
  would have, because it was offered as a credential.
- Logistics are real. Wagons do not climb roadless hills; goods unload somewhere; somebody paid the
  carriage.
- When inventing a process, invent its *failures* too. The eleven housings before the one that held
  do more for credibility than any description of the one that held.

## MODULE 9 — CHARACTER FUNCTION & THE ENSEMBLE WEB

- **Want vs. need, quietly.** The narrator's want is a home; his need is to stop
  garrisoning his grief. The book's deep structure is the want succeeding while the need
  goes unmet, until the need is forced (letter, chest). Secondary leads get the same
  two-line treatment: Tomas wants the sound explained, needs to not be alone with it;
  Lira wants the valley safe, needs her omissions to be a choice she can live with.
  A character whose want and need are the same line is a prop.
- **Arcs measured in idiom.** Secondary growth registers as a shift in each character's
  OWN grammar, not in new behavior wholesale: Jorin's arc is what he stops asking;
  Lira's is what she omits; Tomas's is where he stands. When planning a character's
  beat, ask what their signature move looks like one degree warmer.
- **The web, not the wheel.** Ensemble warmth requires pairs that exclude the
  protagonist: Jorin–Tomas over deferred iron, Brenna–Lira across the lane, Cael–Soren's
  bickering, Bess reading Hal. At least one spoke-less beat per part — two villagers
  relating to each other with the narrator only watching — or the village collapses into
  a wheel with the wizard at the hub.
- **Orthogonal traits.** Every recurring character carries one trait orthogonal to their
  function (the builder aligns objects to table edges; the investigator's courtesies;
  the joiner's winter's-excuse pride). When adding cast, assign the orthogonal trait
  before the functional one.
- **Children are people** (house rule, restated as craft): a child character gets a
  logic system, not a cuteness system — Soren's is weight-and-seriousness, Nell's is
  category-audit ("that's not really a job"). Test every child line: could an adult in
  this book say it? If yes, it isn't the child's line yet.

## MODULE 10 — MYSTERY CADENCE & THE FAIR-PLAY LEDGER

The undercurrent is a mystery plot wearing weather, and mystery has its own contract:

- **Escalating specificity, never repetition.** Each surfacing tells the reader
  something FINER: felt → timed → responsive → learning → directional → grammatical →
  almost a word → the word. Two beats at the same specificity is one beat too many.
- **Fair play.** Everything the payoff requires is on the page before it pays (the
  compass, the corner, the bearing, the practice of a word all pre-exist the needle).
  Nothing the payoff requires is FLAGGED as required (the blind-seed law). These two
  rules together are the whole art: visible to memory, invisible to prediction.
- **Reader-superior tension is a loan.** Whenever the reader knows more than the
  narrator (Lira's ledger, the hammer pause), interest accrues — and the debt comes due:
  the gap must eventually close on the page or be formally banked with its closure
  named for a later book. An unserviced superiority gap reads as the author forgetting.
- **The recontextualizing twist** (the book's chosen kind): the reveal must make every
  prior mystery-beat mean MORE, not different — a lens, not a rug-pull. Test: after the
  twist, could a reader re-read any undercurrent beat and find it richer? Any beat the
  twist invalidates rather than deepens is a defect to fix before the reveal.
- **One question retired, one opened.** Whenever a mystery question closes, its
  successor is already standing (the needle answers "what does the hill want" and opens
  "what is behind the door" in the same breath). The series engine is this relay; never
  close the last open question of a book.

## MODULE 11 — THE COZY CONTRACT, GRADUATE EDITION

The genre's working canon, read as craft, yields harder rules than "low stakes":

- **Low stakes raise the bar; they do not lower it.** With no dread of death, every
  character interaction must carry the load explosions carry elsewhere — there is
  nothing to hide weak scenes behind. Budget accordingly: the genre's action scene is a
  negotiation, a confession, a harvest, a kindness timed exactly right.
- **Comfort is the cast's treatment of each other, not the absence of trouble.** What
  readers name as "cozy" on inspection is how the community holds its members when
  trouble comes (the lantern chain, "eat it while it's warm"). Trouble is therefore
  REQUIRED — it is the pressure that makes the holding visible. A trouble-free stretch
  is spending the genre's capital without earning it.
- **Build the threat to paradise in the background while paradise is assembled.** The
  genre's classic flaw is a warm front half with nothing under it. This book's
  undercurrent-as-weather is the correct solution; the rule is to keep the background
  thread's instrument visible during warmth (Module 10, refusal-beats) so the late
  tightening feels like arrival, not import.
- **One completed satisfaction per chapter** (unchanged from v1, now with a taxonomy):
  competence (a thing made that works), kindness (landed, not announced), recognition (a
  person accurately seen), abundance (food, warmth, harvest), and justice-in-miniature
  (a fairness restored). Rotate them; five competence-satisfactions in a row reads as a
  craft manual.
- **The ending owes warmth ON the page.** Cozy endings resolve into community, meal, or
  hearth even when the plot resolves into wonder. The coda's kettle is load-bearing
  genre furniture; protect it in every revision.
- **Friction without villainy; sweetness with a held-back tenth** (v1 §1 and §7 stand,
  promoted to contract terms).

### OVERRULED — standard advice, and what this book does instead

| Standard advice | Why it is standard | What this book does |
|---|---|---|
| Every scene needs conflict | Conflict guarantees a scene has a reason to exist | Every scene needs **change**. Name what changed: a fact, a relationship, an understanding, or the temperature. Conflict is one way to change something; it is not the only way. |
| Escalate stakes each act | Rising stakes counteract reader fatigue | Stakes stay **village-sized**. What grows is **implication** — the same small thing meaning more because more is attached to it. A burned barn matters because of who comes up the hill for it. |
| Open with a hook, in medias res | Buys attention before it is earned | Open on **consequence** (Module 2). The hook is the narrator's voice and the reader's trust in it. A hook that outruns the register is a promise the book will not keep. |
| Cut anything that doesn't advance plot | Prevents flab | Atmosphere **is** the product. Cut the **empty** chapter — one that neither moves nor deepens anything — never the quiet one. A chapter whose work is texture earns its place if the attention is alive on the page. |
| The protagonist must drive the action | Passive protagonists bore | This book's problems are solved by **patience and other people**. That is the thesis, not a defect. Emlyn's competence is in *asking*; the plot is the valley answering. |
| Increase try/fail cycles | Manufactures tension | Increase **cost and reciprocity**. The failure that matters is not "he tried and failed" but "he got it and it cost someone something." |
| Give the antagonist page time | Externalises the conflict | There is no villain on stage. The pressures are money, weather, distance, and grief. Keep the distant institutions as **weather**, not as enemies. |

**The test that replaces the conflict test:** at the end of a chapter, can you say in one sentence
what is different now that was not different before, and does at least one person feel it? If yes, the
chapter is doing its job however quiet it is.

## MODULE 12 — THE RETROSPECTIVE TELLER (the frame, mastered)

The memoir frame is a craft system of its own, with four instruments:

- **The two clocks.** Every sentence chooses: the man THEN (immediate, inside events) or
  the teller NOW (beside them, weighing). The mix is the book's pulse — mostly THEN in
  scenes, NOW rationed to sequels and chapter seams. Drift toward NOW is the frame's
  besetting sin; audit any chapter where the teller's commentary exceeds a few beats.
- **Hindsight weights; it never predicts.** The teller may confess what he failed to
  notice, what he has turned over since, what he still cannot place — all of which
  deepen. He may not promise significance ahead ("I would learn," "not for the last
  time") — which flattens. This is the telegraph ban derived from first principles: the
  frame's suspense is "how did this ordinary day become the story he must tell," and
  prediction spends that suspense at a discount.
- **The addressee is a resource.** A book told TO someone can withhold as intimacy
  (things the listener already knows), summarize as courtesy, and pay debts at frame
  level (the kettle line). Use sparingly — the addressee surfacing more than a few times
  per book turns the novel into a letter — but remember it exists when a payoff wants a
  register no scene can reach.
- **Unreliability, the honest kind.** This teller does not lie; he arranges, omits, and
  audits himself late (the drawer, the smooth dinner-party sentence). His
  self-corrections are trust-BUILDING events; schedule at least one per part where the
  teller catches his own account bending and straightens it on the page.

## MODULE 13 — REVISION AS DIAGNOSTICS (the passes)

The existing engine (lint, blind-seed probe, editorial read, structural diagnostic)
stands. Three passes join it, each answerable with page numbers:

- **The tension pass** (Module 3): sample cold pages across the manuscript; each must
  yield a sentence naming what the reader is uneasy about on that page. Log the blanks;
  fix by adding opposing current, not event.
- **The distance pass** (Module 4): mark the psychic-distance level at every scene's
  open, peak, and close; flag jolts and flat-lines (whole chapters at one distance).
- **The emotion pass** (Module 5): for each major beat, name the emotion the READER is
  meant to produce (not the character's), and identify what on the page produces it. "The
  character's stated feeling" is a failing answer at a threshold beat.
