# EXEMPLARS — worked examples from the book itself

**Purpose.** `storycraft.md` states the principles. This file shows them working, using passages from this
manuscript, so drafting has in-house models rather than abstract rules. Every entry names the
*mechanism*, not the effect: "this is good" teaches nothing; "this works because the answer changes
the question" is portable.

**Citation convention.** Chapter number plus a distinctive searchable phrase. **Never line numbers** —
this manuscript is actively revised and line numbers rot within a session. If a quote no longer
locates, the passage was edited; re-find it or retire the entry.

---

## SCENE AND SUMMARY (storycraft.md Module 2 (scene design))

### Summary used correctly, as transit — Ch 19
> search: *"came up the frontier road in pieces, on eleven wagons"*

Eleven wagons over a season compressed into a clause. This is what summary is for: the transit is
real, matters as a fact, and would be tedious played out. Note it delivers the reader to the door of
the scene that follows rather than standing in for it.

### The load-bearing beat played in real time — Ch 10
> search: *"Second year, a tenth of what it yields. Third year and after, a fifth."*

The tenancy bargain is the money arc's foundation, and it happens in voices, in a muddy field, with a
wife in a doorway putting a figure down where it can be looked at. Rendered as summary this would be
one sentence and the book would be poorer by a character.

### Refusal to look, as a form of watering — Ch 17
> search: *"a ledger filling while every other ledger emptied"*

The stone is kept alive across a long silence by the narrator declining to read it. Costs one
sentence, characterizes the refusal, and converts a dormant thread into suspense. Compare §6's note
on watering: the refusal is the cheapest legitimate form.

## THE TURN (storycraft.md Module 2 (the turn))

### The weighing given its own beat — Ch 27
> search: *"He did not answer that at once. He took the door up off the trestles instead"*

Jorin does not reply to the request. He lifts a door alone, stands it against a wall, and only then
speaks. The physical action *is* the deliberation; without it the consent that follows would read as
authorial convenience.

### The settle, landed as image not explanation — Ch 5
> search: *"\"Good,\" he said."*

One word closes a chapter-long build. Nothing explains what it cost the speaker to say or what it
means to hear. The reader performs the subtraction, which is the whole pleasure.

## DIALOGUE AS ACTION (storycraft.md Module 6 (dialogue & subtext))

### The answer that changes the question — Ch 27
> search: *"I'm not giving him to you"*

Asked to release his son to an apprenticeship, Jorin refuses the framing rather than the request:
*"I'm letting him choose, and that's a different article altogether, and it costs a good deal more."*
He answers a question about permission with a statement about authorship. The gap is the meaning.

### Diction domain under pressure — Ch 25
> search: *"It's built true and it stands other"*

Ren has no vocabulary for the uncanny, so he reaches into carpentry and comes out with something
better than a trained word. Later in the same chapter he refuses the word *wrong* on technical
grounds: *"a fault's got a where."* His trade is doing the thinking, which is what a diction domain
is for.

### Understatement proportional to cost — Ch 26
> search: *"\"Than the sound.\""*

Three years of private doubt, corrected in two words. Tomas is the book's proof that the least
talkative character can carry the most freight, provided the narration does not rush in to gloss him.

### The domestic deflection — Ch 27
> search: *"It'll be dry"*

A mother watches her eldest son's life change at her table and says the food is drying out. The line
does three jobs: it releases the room's tension, it characterizes her entirely, and it declines to
name a feeling the reader can already feel.

### Working talk under pressure — Ch 30
> search: *"House is gone. Study."*

Four words establish triage, command, and the fact that everyone present already understands the
situation. In a crisis, competent people do not explain; the compression *is* the characterization.

### Comic beat that is also characterization — Ch 28
> search: *"Plants don't lie. It's why I prefer them."*

Brenna's humour is never a garnish; it is her worldview stated flatly. Per storycraft.md Module 11 (the OVERRULED table), humour in this
register is load-bearing, not decorative.

## CAUSALITY AND MOMENTUM (storycraft.md Module 2 (cause before effect))

### Consequence opening, strong form — Ch 18
> search: *"What I did about it took the rest of that autumn"*

Opens by pointing directly at the prior chapter's verdict. The reader is oriented and moving inside
one clause. Compare the counter-example below.

### The cheap repair, in place — Ch 16
> search: *"Penworth had said it on the hillside in the summer"*

This chapter previously opened *"The bottoms came in over nine days at the front of autumn"* — a pure
calendar entry. The cause was already on the page two chapters earlier and simply was not claimed.
The repair invented nothing. **This is the model for every calendar opening you find.**

## SETUP AND PAYOFF (storycraft.md Module 1 (promise/progress/payoff))

### Banking, stated twice and meant — Ch 25
> search: *"I don't know yet what it means"*

Said, then repeated deliberately at the chapter's close. The repetition is the banking mechanism: it
tells the reader the author is holding the thread on purpose, converting an unanswered question from
an itch into anticipation.

### The long payoff, unannounced — Ch 30
> search: *"a purpose I had never once been able to name to myself"*

The way is laid in Ch 20 for no stated reason. Ten chapters later the village runs up it in the dark
to a fire. Nothing in Ch 20 hints; nothing in Ch 30 explains. The reader assembles it.

### Payment at moral cost — Ch 10 → Ch 18
> search: *"Men find reasons"* (Ch 10) and *"waiting eleven years already"* (Ch 10)

Bess Wray's three words plant a doubt about landlords that Ch 18 then pays by having the narrator
prove her partly right. The best payoffs cost the protagonist something.

## INFORMATION MANAGEMENT (storycraft.md Module 10 (mystery cadence))

### Dramatic irony without a threat — Ch 21

The reader watches Lira file a report Emlyn will not learn of for a long time. No danger appears on
the page, and yet every subsequent quiet chapter carries a charge it did not have before. **This is
the strongest tension device available to a low-conflict book.**

## POV AS INSTRUMENT (storycraft.md Module 4A (the witness position))

### Hindsight used for judgment, not foreshadowing — Ch 10
> search: *"Paperwork can wait. People, as it turned out, had been waiting eleven years already."*

Retrospect is spent on self-indictment rather than on plot. The narrator's older self is used to
convict his younger self, which costs no tension because it points at character, not at events.

### Wonder rendered as bookkeeping — Ch 24
> search: *"I audited a hill."*

The book's largest supernatural finding arrives in the diction of an accounts clerk. The register
holds the wonder down to the ground, and the specificity of the method is what makes the finding
credible.

## THE AUTHORITY OF THE SPECIFIC (storycraft.md Module 8 (the authority of the specific))

### A tolerance nobody can draw — Ch 22
> search: *"\"Gauge,\" he said."*

Emlyn cannot specify "half a hair" on a drawing; Tomas files a slot in scrap iron until the glass
drops through under its own weight. The problem is real, the solution is physical, and the reader
extends the credit earned here to everything stranger in the book.

### Invented process, with its failures included — Ch 32
> search: *"eleven housings before the one that held"*

Ten failures stacked on a bench do more for the instrument's credibility than any description of the
one that worked.

### Method rendered honestly, including self-distrust — Ch 25
> search: *"He will negotiate with it."*

Writing down beforehand what a null result would look like, because *"a man who has not written his
own disappointment down beforehand will not recognise it when it arrives."* Competence displayed is
the cozy contract's primary engine (storycraft.md Module 11 (the OVERRULED table)).

### The principle stated once, then never again — Ch 8
> search: *"the same manners work on daylight as on grief"*

The book's thesis, landed in a commerce chapter, in one clause, and never explained. The thesis is
enacted everywhere else and stated almost nowhere.

# COUNTER-EXAMPLES — failures caught in this manuscript, and their fixes

These teach faster than the successes because the defect and the repair are both in the author's own
voice.

### Outcome revealed before the scene that earns it — Ch 9 / Ch 10

**Was:** Ch 9 closed on *"my new tenants sat up over their seed."* Ch 10 then opened *"I have got
ahead of myself with the house. Go back to the front of that spring,"* and dramatized the winning of
those tenants.
**Defect:** the reader learns the outcome, then watches the negotiation for it. The Wray bargain — one
of the book's best scenes — plays with its tension already spent, and the narrator must announce a
rewind to cover the inversion.
**Fix:** the clause was cut; the bottoms now lie *"dark and empty and waiting on me."* **No reorder was
needed once the spoiler was gone.**
**Lesson:** check every chapter's final image against the next chapter's job.

### Calendar opening with the cause two chapters back — Ch 16

**Was:** *"The bottoms came in over nine days at the front of autumn."*
**Defect:** the season arrives; nothing causes the chapter. It reads as *and then*.
**Fix:** repointed to Penworth's existing ultimatum and spade condition, making the harvest an
installment that must be cut by hand.
**Lesson:** the cause is nearly always already written. Point at it.

### Forward-flash that spends the next chapter's discovery — Ch 6

**Was:** a clause noting that the lamps he came to sell *"drink another well entirely."*
**Defect:** pre-announces the daylight invention that Ch 8 spends its whole middle discovering,
converting a discovery into an appointment (storycraft.md Module 4A (the witness position)).
**Fix:** clause cut; the iterative R&D arc it sat inside was kept intact.
**Lesson:** a forward-flash is nearly always deletable without loss, because it adds nothing the
later chapter will not deliver better.

### The orphan — Ch 21

**Defect:** a packet of old letters tied with a bootlace, and a finished "season" of Lira's life
involving someone at the office, planted with real weight and then never referenced again in fifteen
chapters or the coda. Invisible to every per-chapter check: the chapter that plants it is excellent,
and the chapters that fail to pay it do not know it exists.
**Fix:** one deliberate touch that banks it as a door kept shut on purpose.
**Lesson:** this is what `state/thread-ledger.md` exists to prevent. PAID or BANKED are both fine;
ORPHANED is the only failure.

### The crescendo that outran the register — Ch 27

**Was:** the apprenticeship scene closed on a comparison to the Conclave, a forward reference to "the
tower to come," and a count of the words the boy had answered with.
**Defect:** three separate escalations stacked on a beat that had already landed. Explanatory tail
plus foreshadow plus self-admiration.
**Fix:** *"and I sat very still in a joiner's warm kitchen, and did not trust myself to speak first."*
**Lesson:** when a beat has landed, stop. The paragraph after the good line usually explains the good
line.

### Texture collapse invisible to per-chapter review — Ch 20–32

**Defect:** dialogue share fell from 13–31% across Parts I–III to 0–2%, and chapter size from ~3,900
words to ~1,800, across the entire final movement. Every one of those chapters passed its own review.
**Fix:** the span was expanded and re-voiced; median chapter size now matches the front half.
**Lesson:** no per-chapter tool can see this, which is what `tools/span-check.sh` exists for. When
it runs is set by the mode (`modes/developmental.md` makes it mandatory after any restructure);
this file only shows why it is needed.
