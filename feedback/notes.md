## Chapter Six: First Light

> My first attempt at the working itself was a failure, and an instructive one, and I mention it because a story would have you believe such things come to a man whole and shining, and they do not. I built the first version to draw. To reach frankly into the hill’s song and pull up a good draught of it and make a bright light, a proper light, a light to be proud of. And the hill did to that lamp precisely what it had done to me the morning I went up the crown wanting to take something: nothing at all. The glass stayed dark. The working sat in it sound and elegant and greedy, and the hill declined to be at home to it, and I sat in the dark I had failed to lift and laughed, because I had known better and done the greedy thing anyway, which is the oldest failing in my trade and mine more than most men’s. I unpicked it and built it smaller and asked for less and left the hill more room to give what it chose, and that was the version that woke. A lamp that asks a little of a thing is given it. A lamp that demands a great deal gets a dark room and a lesson. I have come to think there is no working I have ever done on that hill that was not, underneath, the same working in different clothes, done over and over: the one called asking.
