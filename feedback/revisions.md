## Chapter One: Ardenmoor

ORIGINAL:
> There was no thread of fate in it, whatever a story would want. I did not come to Ardenmoor drawn by anything grander than an errand. I came because Penworth had an unerring eye and I had learned to trust it over my own; because it was spring, and I had spent the whole of the winter shut up in a quiet Vethmark townhouse with the spare rooms closed and sheeted, keeping well out of the way of the noise and the standing crowd of small obligations that a city presses on a man whether he asks for them or not, and had barely marked the season going by; and because there was nowhere else in the wide world I would rather have been, which in those days was less a boast than a fairly bleak measure of how small my life had got. *A hill of no worth and considerable quiet.* When I read that, it sounded like very nearly the only thing left that I wanted.

REVISED:
> There was no thread of fate in it, whatever a story would want. I did not come to Ardenmoor drawn by anything grander than an errand.
