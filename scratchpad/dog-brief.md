# THE DOG — the single source of truth for this thread

Every drafter works from this file and invents NOTHING about her that is not in it. Eighteen
chapters are being written by agents who cannot see each other; this file is the only thing keeping
her one animal.

---

## 1. WHAT SHE IS

**A sheepdog bitch with no flock.** That is the whole idea, and every beat is a variation on it.
Emlyn's hill carries no sheep. She is a working animal that cannot do the one thing she is for,
kept by a man the valley cannot place either — and Ch 16 has a seven-year-old ask him
*"So what are you **for**, then?"* over a plate of beans, to which his answer is *"I'm the one who's
allowed to waste a great deal of time being wrong."* **That rhyme is the thread's spine and it is
NEVER stated. Not once. No character remarks on it.**

**Name: FLY.** Named by Soren in Ch 14, not before. She is SEEN in Ch 9 and NAMED five chapters
later (`session-locks.md`: people are seen first and named later, as interaction earns it).
Before Ch 14 she is "the pup", "the bitch", "she". No forward-looking asides — never "who would
become", never "I did not know then".

**Appearance, fixed:** black and white; small for a collie; one ear that stands and one that does
not. That is all. Do not add markings, eyes, or a description of her face. She is characterised by
what she DOES, exactly as every human in this book is characterised by what their hands do.

**Mother:** the quarryman's bitch, from `05-good.md:239-247`, already on the page. The quarryman
stays UNNAMED — he is "the quarryman" throughout, as he already is.

## 2. WHAT SHE DOES — the fixed behaviour set

Draw from these. Do not invent new ones.

- **She gathers what does not need gathering.** Tools left on the grass, boots, the men at their
  lunch, Soren. A sheepdog with no sheep works whatever is in front of her. This is the running
  bit, and it is dry: it is stated as a fact and walked away from.
- **She lies at the EDGE of work, facing it.** Never in it, never underfoot. At the outrun
  position, though the book never uses that word.
- **She will not go down the eleven cellar steps.** Planted Ch 13, pays Ch 32. It is a dog and a
  dark stone stair. It is NOT magic.
- **She sleeps straight through the hill's two hours.** The dusk pulse that Soren and Tomas can
  hear does not move her at all. Use this sparingly — once, maybe twice in the book.
- **She is silent.** She barks rarely and it is never cute. If she makes a noise it is because
  something is actually there.

**The valley's projection, and the only joke this thread runs.** Ardenmoor decides that the
wizard's dog must be uncanny, and reads meaning into everything she does. Emlyn knows exactly what
she is and gives up correcting them. **The joke is aimed at EMLYN and at the valley's certainty —
never at the dog, and never at the hill** (voice.md §7.2 rule 5). She is never the punchline.

## 3. THE HARD PROHIBITIONS

Breaking any of these fails the chapter.

1. **She senses nothing.** Ever. No reaction to the hum, the pulses, the corner, the chest, the
   cellar, the compass. `story-bible.md`: Book One carries evidence and never the conclusion. She
   is not an instrument. There is **no tuned or listening instrument before Ch 25** and she must
   not become one — that would spend Ch 25's title beat and pre-empt Ren's whole function.
2. **She is never the alarm.** Snow owns that. Ch 30 opens *"I woke to Snow, screaming"* and it is
   the payoff of a four-year seed. Fly never wakes him, never warns him, never notices first.
3. **She is safe, always.** Content covenant: *animals are safe — in a catastrophe the horse is
   saved first and the saving is shown.* She is never hurt, never ill, never in danger, never
   threatened, and she does not die. Not on the page, not off it, not implied for later.
4. **No interiority.** First person past; we see her from outside only. Never "she knew", "she
   understood", "she seemed to think". Observable behaviour only.
5. **No intention, no destiny.** She was not sent, did not choose him in any meaningful sense, was
   not waiting. She is a pup that ended up in a cart.
6. **No whimsy.** `storycraft.md`: *no cute creatures.* If a sentence about her could sit unchanged
   in a chattier, sweeter book, cut it. No exclamation, no baby-talk, no "little", no comic
   mugging. Underplay is the reaction system here: the withheld reaction IS the reaction.
7. **Zero humour in Ch 26–32.** Part IV is dry on purpose and LOCKED (voice.md §7.3a). She may be
   PRESENT there; she may not be funny there. Ch 31 and Ch 32 are a fence, not a budget.
8. **Nothing comic within sight of** the chest, the letter, the sticking drawer, or the bedroom.
9. **Do not spend the protected callbacks on her**: *"for company"*, the lamplight, and *"see what
   the morning would bring"* are plotted closers owned by other chapters. Especially "for company"
   — it is registry-owned and it is the obvious wrong choice for a dog.
10. **She spends ZERO aphorisms.** Part IV is already three over its allowance of two. No maxims,
    no wisdom, no summing-up about dogs or loyalty or company. If a line of the thread teaches the
    reader something, cut it.
11. **Never appears in:** `21-letters-going-both-ways.md` (Lira's head, Emlyn absent),
    `24-the-reading.md`, any interlude, or `32b-coda.md` (byte-identical, untouchable).

## 5. THE TEST FOR EVERY SENTENCE

> Would this sentence survive if the dog were a wheelbarrow?

If the line's only content is warmth — if it exists so the reader will feel fond — cut it. The
warmth in this book is generated by precision, labour, weather, courtesy and things done properly.
The exact sentence about the kettle IS the coziness. Write her the way the book writes hands.
