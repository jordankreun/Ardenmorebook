# The Tower of Ardenmoor

## Book One

### Prologue

I have got to the age where a man begins to write things down. Not because the memory is going; mine never has, and I have had a good deal longer than most men to put it to the test. It is only that a life, run on long enough, comes to a point where writing a thing down is simply what you do with it, the way you finally mend the gate you have spent years climbing over.

There is this, besides. Once in a great while, in some inn a long way from anywhere, I hear a story told that I half recognize, the way you might recognize your own coat on a man three sizes too big for it. There are not many of them. I am not, whatever you may have been led to expect, a man they sing about, and I took a fair amount of quiet care over the years that it should stay so. But a few tales got loose all the same, and time and retelling have knocked them so far out of true that the man in them is half myth and half plain nonsense. I would not know him to nod to in the road, and, if I am honest, I do not much care for him. I hold the tellers no grudge. A good story is a kind of coin, and it is meant to be spent. It only seems a poor thing to leave nothing behind but those few, and so, if only so that the truth of it should exist somewhere, in one place, I mean to set down what actually happened. Or near enough. I will not pretend the years have warmed no words and softened no faces; fondness does that, and I was fond of very nearly all of them, and they are gone now, every one but me. Still, the shape of it is true, and the shape is the thing that matters.

So I will start where the true account starts, and not where the stories like to. They love to open on a wonder, or on a boy. There is no wonder in it, and there is no boy. There is only a man, at the tired end of a bad few years, with a good deal more skill than sense and no particular reason to be anywhere at all. That is who this is about, and I will ask you to go easy with him. He did not know yet how any of it would turn out. I do, and that is the one advantage I have ever held over him, and the one I would most gladly give back.
