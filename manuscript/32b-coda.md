# The Tower of Ardenmoor

## Book One

### Interlude IV

Deep in the year's floor, at the bottom of the longest night, the hill says its word, and the word comes right.

The small closed weight in the dry corner stands open. The bright thing the hill has sung toward through bones and roots and two years of patient practice lies awake at last in the great weight's warm hands, and turns, and answers the one way it was ever going to answer, the way the hill has waited out a village's whole name to see.

Down.

The hill holds its deepest pulse one long moment past its measure, the way still water holds a dropped star.

*At last.*

### From the same later journal

So there it is, the start of it, as I promised you, and you will notice I have ended it exactly where you always stop me when I tell it aloud, with the needle pointing at the floor and your master on his knees in the cold, too old already to be kneeling on stone and too far gone in wonder to notice.

You asked me once, in your patient way, why the story could not begin with the door. Now you have your answer: because the door is not the beginning. The beginning is a bowl of soup, and a hill nobody wanted, and every hand that came up a bad road to help build the rest of it. The door comes after, the way it came after, and it can wait for the next volume the way it waited under my floor, which it will do gladly, having had the practice.

Set the kettle on, Soren. The rest wants telling by the fire.
